# Hired Not Tired marketing claim

**Status:** Released copy with unvalidated messaging hypotheses. No target-user research or message-testing participants have been recruited or contacted.

## Locked claim hierarchy

- **Brand:** Hired Not Tired.
- **Brand-level claim / H1:** “You are more than your CV.”
- **Campaign promise / H2:** “Show employers the value they can't see.”
- **Supporting copy:** “Discover your strengths. Find opportunities that fit. Apply with confidence.”

This hierarchy is the customer-facing message. Do not add AI/agent jargon, guaranteed outcomes, employer-visibility promises, or claims that an application will be noticed.

## Five-second explanation for judges

Hired Not Tired helps job seekers see the value beyond their CV, find roles that fit, and prepare more relevant applications.

## Twenty-second pitch

**Problem:** Experienced professionals can be reduced to a list of job titles, making their wider value difficult to communicate.

**Promise:** Hired Not Tired helps you show employers the value beyond your CV.

**How it works:** You discover and confirm your strengths, explore opportunities that fit, and prepare a relevant application while remaining in control of what you share.

**Outcome:** The intended outcome is a clearer account of what you offer and a more confident, relevant application. This is a product hypothesis, not a promise of interviews or employment.

## First-test target audience

Experienced professionals who are actively job searching or considering a move.

## Customer-facing message versus internal product strategy

The customer-facing message is limited to the locked claim hierarchy and the plain-language explanations above. It should help a visitor understand who the product is for and why it may matter to them.

Internal product strategy concerns application packages, profile evidence, implementation architecture, provider capabilities, workflow design and delivery sequencing. Those details guide the team but must not displace the simple customer promise or turn the landing page into a feature inventory. Customer copy must only describe capabilities that actually exist and must preserve truthful sign-in, demo and application-handoff states.

## Hypotheses and unvalidated assumptions

### Hypotheses

- **H1 — comprehension:** After five seconds, target users can identify experienced job seekers as the audience and explaining value beyond a CV as the benefit.
- **H2 — relevance:** The message feels relevant to experienced professionals who are searching or considering a move.
- **H3 — credibility:** “Show employers the value they can't see” is perceived as a credible communication benefit, not a guarantee of visibility, attention, interviews or employment.
- **H4 — emotional resonance:** “You are more than your CV” helps participants feel that their broader experience and strengths are recognized.
- **H5 — action:** Clearer positioning increases hero-to-sign-in/demo engagement without making the fictional demo appear to be real authentication.

### Unvalidated assumptions

- The target audience experiences the stated problem strongly enough to care.
- Participants interpret “value they can't see” as the candidate's less-visible strengths, not as hidden data collection or employer surveillance.
- The promise is sufficiently distinct from conventional CV and job-search products.
- The message motivates a meaningful next step rather than only passive agreement.
- The two proposed secondary promises differ enough to produce useful directional feedback.

None of these hypotheses or assumptions has been validated. No experiment has been run.

## Validation experiment — pending

1. Recruit **10–15 people** from the first-test target audience for an **unmoderated five-second test**.
2. Show **only the hero** for five seconds: H1, one secondary promise, supporting copy and the existing actions. Do not show benefit cards, product demonstrations or explanatory notes.
3. Hide the hero, then ask:
   - What does this product do?
   - Who is it for?
   - What benefit would you expect from it?
4. Compare the two secondary promises:
   - **A:** “Show employers the value they can't see”
   - **B:** “Turn your experience into your next opportunity”
5. Randomize which promise each participant sees. Record exposure assignment and avoid showing both versions before the initial comprehension questions.
6. Record **comprehension, relevance, credibility and emotional resonance separately**. Use short independent ratings for the latter three and retain verbatim answers for comprehension. Do not collapse them into one score.

### Decision rule

Keep the promise only if **at least 8/10 test participants can correctly explain both the target user and the benefit**. For samples larger than 10, apply the same 80% directional threshold. This is a directional product-learning rule, not statistical proof. If the threshold is not met, rewrite the promise and retest; do not explain away unclear answers or relabel them as comprehension.

Review relevance, credibility and emotional resonance independently before choosing a winner. A promise that scores well on resonance but poorly on comprehension or credibility has not passed.

## Metrics after launch — not instrumented by this release

Measure the following funnel steps using existing consent and configuration boundaries:

- Hero-to-sign-in/demo CTA click-through, reported separately for sign-in and demo.
- Demo start.
- First meaningful profile insight confirmed.
- Job viewed.
- Application package started.
- Application package completed.

Do not instrument these events or send personal data to third parties without the necessary existing consent and configuration. This release adds no analytics provider and reports no performance results.
